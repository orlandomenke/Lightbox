# Q255 · A brush's alternate on Alt, and rewetting as a brush — **answered 2026-10-12**

**Answered: Alt and Ctrl+Alt each give the brush in hand an alternate brush; Watercolor and Ink wash get rewetting on Alt and a blot on Ctrl+Alt; rewetting is a bleed that dilutes, with pigment gathering where the water stopped.**

Raised by: the owner, after the last two media became recipes: *"Let's first focus on rewetting Gouache, watercolor. Perhaps we add a generic brush short cut (is alt still free?) that could get different uses based on the brush?"* Earlier the owner had ruled out a wet/dry state for paint: reworking is *"a separate brush specifically for rewetting. Or a specific special mode for the brush."*

What it blocks: the rework brushes on the roadmap (water, blot, damp brush), and how any of them is reached.

## What was asked, and the answers

**1. The key.** Alt was not free: at pen-down with the brush it erases with the brush in hand. Ctrl is the held eyedropper and Shift a straight segment. **Answered: Alt becomes "this brush's alternate", and a brush that names none keeps erasing on Alt** (recommended) - then, with two rework brushes on the table, *"alt rewetting, ctrl + alt blot?"*. So a brush has two alternates: one on Alt, one on Ctrl+Alt. Ctrl+Alt at pen-down was the eyedropper (Ctrl won); it stays that for a brush with no second alternate. Costs accepted: Watercolor and Ink wash lose Alt-erase (E still erases).

**2. What an alternate is.** **Answered: another brush** (recommended), named by the brush in the editor, painted with at the current size; the alternates are also brushes in the picker. The stroke is recorded as an ordinary stroke of that brush - nothing new in the file. The alternative was fixed built-in actions per medium, which would have left made and imported brushes without one.

**3. The blot.** The owner's own practice: *"In real life in wash work I use a paper towel to dab the edges so I can create subtle gradients, especially layering ink washes ... the more pressure I use, the more ink I remove, with a minimum."* Three rows on a sheet. **Answered: lifts up to 80%, firmer edge, more towel texture.** It is an eraser whose stroke opacity is its ceiling, flow on pressure, on the Fibre sheet - no new engine work. *"Not sure how it would work on the other paints"*: left open; the same brush can be any brush's alternate.

**4. Rewetting Watercolor.** The first sheet (smears and a blur) was wrong, and the owner said why with a reference: *"the rewetting is more a bleed/spread and diluting the pigment."* A second sheet as a picture prototype. **Answered: cell 6** - spreads far, cloudy, pigment gathers where the water stopped - *"but I think the pooling should be more dynamic and irregular. But that is in general I think."* The last sentence is a note about pooling everywhere (the wet edge too), not only this brush.

**5. How it responds.** **Answered: pressure sets how wet** (recommended); going over a place again spreads it further; size sets how far it can spread.

**6. Gouache.** Five rows of a damp brush. **Answered: "2 with 5 I guess combined"** - a short drag with the brush tip, and colours blending where it passes.

## What it costs

- A new kind of brush in the engine for the bleed: no existing one spreads paint with a soft edge, thins it and shows the paper in it. It has to land under the pen and be what is kept at pen-up, like every other mark.
- Two more keys on the preset record, absent unless a brush names an alternate.
- Alt-erase is no longer universal, which the manual has to say.

## Later the same thread, 2026-10-13

**The water brush's look.** Rendered by the engine this time, six cells. **Answered: cell 2, as built** - spreads about half a brush width, thins a fifth, cloudy pools, a moderate line where the water stopped.

**Push and pull.** The owner asked whether rewetting also means pushing and pulling the paint. It does not: the bleed is even in every direction. A strength for dragging paint in the direction of travel is planned on the same brush, not built.

**The damp brush for Gouache.** "2 with 5 combined" cannot be one brush as asked - a smudge brush either drags or blends - so a second sheet showed the two rows again and three ways of combining them. **Answered: "the same but gentler"** (blends through the tip, short reach, soft edge, a few passes to blend fully). It goes pale where it runs onto bare paper, which was said before the choice.

**The Blot on Gouache.** **Answered: yes, the same Blot on Ctrl+Alt** (recommended).
